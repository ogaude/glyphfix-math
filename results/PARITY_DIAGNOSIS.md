# HF bf16 vs llama.cpp/Ollama output gap: diagnosis (p4-full-1)

Question: on the 136 dev-mini crops, the HF merged bf16 model and the GGUF served by Ollama give different greedy text on 18 crops (quick 261016-vwi). The bf16 GGUF agrees with Q8_0 on 16 of 18, so text-weight quantization is not the cause. What in the llama.cpp/Ollama path causes the gap, and does it matter for Ollama-scored results?

Evidence: `evidence/parity_diagnosis.json` (per-crop rows and arm counts). Scratch code and raw outputs stayed in the session scratchpad; nothing registered was touched (`data/benchmark`, `data/models/REGISTRY.json`, the `glyphfix-math-lora-q8:p4-full-1` tag). No Ollama tag was created. Ollama's bundled `llama-server` was started directly, under the guard, and stopped after each run. All GPU jobs ran under `glyphfix_math.guard`, with 16 GiB of VRAM and about 20 GiB of RAM free at the start. The f16 projector of row 9 is a scratch file and is not registered. Software: transformers 5.19.0, torch 2.14.1+rocm7.2, Ollama 0.40.1 (llama.cpp build 631109b34).

## Verdict

The gap is numerical noise at near-ties in greedy decoding, not a bug in the GGUF path. Every one of the 18 divergences starts at a step where HF's two best tokens are tied or nearly tied, and the token llama.cpp continued with is HF's second choice in all 18.

The main mechanism has two parts:

1. HF computes the LM head in bf16. With logits around 20 to 30, bf16 can only represent multiples of 0.125, so two candidate tokens often get exactly the same logit. `torch.argmax` then picks the lower token id (verified on all 31 exact ties in the rerun). llama.cpp returns f32 logits and keeps its intermediate results in f32, so it breaks the same near-tie by the real difference, which is smaller than 0.125.
2. Where the true gap is above the bf16 step but still small (0.004 to 0.16 logit), the remaining numeric differences between the two engines are enough to flip it: bf16 activations in HF against f32 in llama.cpp, a vision tower with bf16 weights in HF against Q8_0 weights in the GGUF path (see the note below), and different kernels. Two measurements show how small these differences are. On aligned steps, the two engines' top-1/top-2 gaps differ by a median of 0.04 logit. llama.cpp also disagrees with itself between its CPU and ROCm backends on 4 of the 18.

Making only HF's LM head fp32 (everything else unchanged) turns 8 of the 18 into exact matches of the llama.cpp text and leaves all 30 control crops unchanged. The other 10 have f32 margins of 0.004 to 0.16 logit at the divergence point.

Most of the flipped pairs are equivalent spellings of the same formula: `}` + ` \,` against `}\,`, `^1` against `^{1}`, `\boldsymbol` against `\bm`, `\left\vert` against `\vert` against `|`, `\ge` against `\geq`. One changes content: c00db734, where HF reads the subscript as `X_i` (matches the ground truth) and llama.cpp reads it as `\tilde{X}_i`. HF's f32 margin there is 0.09; llama.cpp's is 0.52 with the Q8_0 projector and 0.22 with an f16 one. HF with an fp32 vision tower also produces `\tilde{X}_i`. This is the only one of the 18 whose `render_identical` outcome differs between engines on the tuned model. After the first flip the two outputs continue in different styles, which is why some differ for the rest of the line.

Note on the projector: the stock projector blob `fd5acde1`, used by both the Q8_0 and the bf16 GGUF runs, is a Q8_0 mmproj (`general.file_type` 7; linear weights Q8_0, norms, biases and patch embed F32), not an f16 one as the bf16 parity paragraph in `DEV_ANALYSIS.md` assumed. So the GGUF path does quantize the vision tower. Swapping in an f16 projector converted from the merged model (row 9) changes little: 3 of the 18 move to the HF text, 1 moves away from it, and 1 of 30 controls flips at a 0.125 near-tie.

## Ruled out

- **Text-weight quantization:** the bf16 GGUF agrees with Q8_0 on 16 of 18 (quick 261016-vwi).
- **Sampling, stop and length settings:** Ollama's llama-server logs `temp = 0.000` (greedy: top-k/top-p are no-ops), `repeat_penalty = 1.000`, no DRY/mirostat. HF uses `do_sample=False`. Both cap at 1024 new tokens and all 18 stopped on EOS, not on length.
- **Prompt and template:** prompt token counts match on 136/136 for both the tuned and the stock model, and on 48/48 in the direct llama-server reruns. The 14 text tokens around the image match HF's `<|im_start|>user\n<|vision_start|>…<|vision_end|><|im_end|>\n<|im_start|>assistant\n<think>\n\n</think>\n\n`. The first generated tokens (```` ```latex\n ````) agree everywhere until the near-tie.
- **Image preprocessing:** the client sends the `pixels.apply_policy` PNG, whose size is already a multiple of 32 inside the pixel window. HF's processor output equals plain `(x/255 - 0.5)/0.5` patchification to 5.9e-8 on all 18 crops, so the processor does not resize. llama.cpp's `dyn_size` preprocessor copies a same-size image without resampling (`img_tool::resize`, checked in the llama.cpp source), and the projector GGUF carries mean/std 0.5.
- **M-RoPE positions for image tokens:** llama.cpp gives an image chunk `max(nx, ny)` positions. Ollama's log shows text resuming at position 46 after a 13x42 grid and at 21 after a 4x17 grid, which equals HF's `get_rope_index` (start 4 + max side).
- **Projector quantization as the main cause:** the f16 projector moves only 3 of the 18 to the HF text (row 9).
- **A systematic implementation error in the GGUF path** (projector, DeltaNet kernels, rope): such an error would also cause divergences at confident steps. Over 5,520 steps of the 71 matching controls the texts are identical, and every one of the 18 divergences is at a gap of 0.125 logit or less in bf16 and 0.16 or less in f32. A small systematic difference (for example Q8_0 against bf16 vision weights, or kernel accumulation order) acts like noise here and cannot be told apart from it with this test. Any such difference is too small to change a token unless the model is already at a near-tie.

## What remains unknown

- The vision features were not compared tensor by tensor (HF projector output against the llama.cpp mmproj output on a real crop). `llama-mtmd-debug` only takes synthetic square images and is not built here. The visual side was tested end to end instead (rows 6 and 9): it matters at near-ties, but by about as much as the other perturbations.
- Ollama does not log token ids. Token alignment uses a direct rerun of Ollama's bundled llama-server, which reproduces the Ollama bf16 text 18/18 (row 7). The Q8_0 tag's own token-level log-probs were not taken.
- Not tested on page-level inputs. Long outputs pass through more near-ties, so HF and GGUF page transcriptions will diverge more often than crops do. This is expected, not a defect.
- Whether HF with fully fp32 activations would match llama.cpp more often. A full fp32 model does not fit the 16 GB card and would exceed the RAM floor on CPU, so this was not run.

## Effect on Ollama-scored results (Phase 5 and the gates)

- **Head-to-head comparisons stay valid.** Stock and tuned are both scored through the same llama.cpp path, which is deterministic (Phase 2: 104/104 identical across two Ollama runs). The engine adds no bias toward either arm. The stock model shows the same 18/136 HF-vs-Ollama rate as the tuned one.
- **The score effect is small and not directional.** Tuned: 17 of 18 mismatches have the same `render_identical` outcome on both engines, and 1 is right only on HF. Stock: 14 of 18 have the same outcome, 2 are right only on HF and 2 only on Ollama. The aggregate gains agree (HF +0.169, Ollama +0.162 on the dev mini set).
- **Per-crop outcomes on near-tie crops are fragile.** On any engine, a crop decided by a near-tie can flip under a tiny numeric change: Q8_0 vs bf16 GGUF (2 of 18), vision fp32 vs bf16 (8 of 18), Q8_0 vs f16 projector (4 of 18), CPU vs ROCm (4 of 18). Read individual Phase 5 flip-table entries (right-to-wrong in particular) with that in mind; the paired, paper-level statistics are what carry the verdict. Do not mix engines between arms. If HF and GGUF numbers appear side by side, label them as different engines.
- **Nothing to fix in the GGUF or export.** If closer HF-to-GGUF agreement is ever wanted (for example to compare HF mini-eval numbers directly with Ollama), compute the HF LM head in fp32. That costs about 2.5 GB of VRAM, moved 8 of the 18 mismatches to the llama.cpp text and changed none of the 30 controls. It would change the HF evaluation path, so it is not proposed for the frozen Phase 4 numbers. Shipping an f16 projector instead of the stock Q8_0 one is also not worth it on this evidence: it moved 3 of 18 toward HF and 1 away.
